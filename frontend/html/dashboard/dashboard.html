Getting started

Nothing has been set up on this account yet. Benchmarking a model takes three steps, and each one builds on the last — a submission belongs to a model, and a model belongs to a team. Work through them in order and your results will start appearing on this page.

1

Create a team

A team owns the models and submissions made under it, and team membership is how access is shared. Everything else you create will belong to a team, so this comes first. You will be its first member, and you can add colleagues now or any time later — they need to have signed in at least once before they can be added. If you are working on your own, a team of one is perfectly normal.

Create a team
2

Register a model

A model is the thing being evaluated. You register it once, describing what it is — which team owns it, how much temporal context it uses, whether it was pretrained and on which modalities, plus optional links to your code, weights and paper. That description is what makes results comparable between groups, so it is worth filling in properly. You can edit any of it afterwards, and you will submit against the same model many times as your work progresses.

Register a model
3

Make a submission

A submission is a zip of your model's predictions. The folder names inside it tell us which benchmark tasks you are entering — one folder per task — and you then describe how each was run: the training paradigm, the supervision regime, the calibration. Once uploaded, your predictions are scored against held-out test data. Choose whether to publish it, and if you do, it appears on the public leaderboard alongside everyone else's.

Make a submission

After that, this page fills in on its own: your teams and models appear as cards, your most recent submissions as a table, and your best task scores beside them. Scoring is not instant — a submission sits at "pending" until it has been through the pipeline, so scores may take a little while to show up after you upload.
